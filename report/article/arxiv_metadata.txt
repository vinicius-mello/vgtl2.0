Title:
Triangulating Plane Algebraic Curves Intrinsically in $\mathbb{CP}^2$

Authors:
Vinícius Mello (Universidade Federal da Bahia)

Abstract:
A smooth plane algebraic curve is a compact Riemann surface and a real surface of codimension 2 in $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Most methods that mesh it work in an affine chart or over a branched covering of $\mathbb{CP}^1$, and must therefore treat points at infinity, charts or branch cuts separately. We triangulate the curve directly inside $\mathbb{CP}^2$, with no distinguished chart. A 15-vertex triangulation of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, due to Gaifullin, is refined adaptively by Maubach bisection along Fubini-Study geodesics, and the curve is followed from cell to cell by continuation. In each cell, the curve meets the triangular 2-faces in isolated points, which are joined into polygons. Realizing every face as a geodesic cone, rather than as a flat triangle in an affine chart, makes the faces shared by neighboring cells coincide exactly, so the pieces glue into a closed surface, whose topology is checked a posteriori. On our test curves at depth 14, fewer than 0.14% of the cells that meet the curve fail, against 1.8-11.6% with chart-flat faces; the area agrees with Wirtinger's theorem to within 0.15%; and, unlike a box in an affine chart at equal resolution, the mesh covers the whole curve.
